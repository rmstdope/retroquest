Act III — the Phoenix and patience, agreed

The riddle and its answer are unchanged. The Phoenix hints at the Warden of Wisdom when it asks, finishes the thought when it gives the feather, and says a short farewell afterwards. It never names Malakar. Dashed outline = new text; everything else is today's.

1 · Asking the riddle (every talk before the feather)
> talk to phoenix

The phoenix's voice does not come from its beak, but resounds within your mind:

phoenix

What is wisdom when time is short?

The question lingers, bright and weightless, as if the answer might shape the very air around you.

phoenix

I asked this once of the Warden of Wisdom, when he came to me with his two friends. He knew the answer, and did not keep it.

Talking again before answering shows the same text.
2 · A wrong answer
> say fire to phoenix

The phoenix studies you in silence.

phoenix

Is that truly wisdom, when time is short?

Unchanged; the riddle can be answered again.
3a · The right answer, inscription already read
> say patience to phoenix

The phoenix's eyes flare with approval. It plucks a single glowing feather and offers it to you.

phoenix

Wisdom is patience, even when the world burns. Take this, and remember.

phoenix

He gave your answer too. Then time grew short for his friends, and patience was the one thing he had none of. Do not become what he became.

You think of the third name burned deeper than the others on the terraces.

The last line appears only when the Charred Inscription has been read.

4 · Talking again after the feather
> talk to phoenix

The phoenix inclines its head.

phoenix

Remember him when time is short for you.

> say patience to phoenix

The phoenix inclines its head, a knowing warmth in its eyes.

phoenix

You already hold what you have earned.

The riddle is not asked again. Saying anything still gets today's reply.
