Option A — silence is the comforting lie

Like Tilda in Act I. He asks; say truth to pier warden is the virtue. Saying nothing lets him believe the comfort. No second command to learn.

1 · talk to pier warden — after the dead birds have shown
> talk to pier warden

The Pier Warden squints toward the shore. 'I'd mind the high stones if I were you. …'

Pier Warden

'You'll have seen the causeway. Birds by the hundred, and the surf gone grey. I've known bad seasons on this coast, forty years of them. It's only a bad season, isn't it? Tell me the sea will mend on its own.'

His question comes once the curse has shown at the ruins (after the letter). Until then he talks as today.
2a · the truth
> say truth to pier warden

You tell the Pier Warden what you know: this is no season. Something is draining the king, and the sea with him, and it is getting worse.

Pier Warden

'… Aye. I think I knew it, and only wanted someone to say otherwise. Better a hard truth than a soft lie. I'll send word up the coast to keep the boats in.'

✦ Truth — this will be remembered

2b · Elior says nothing, talks again
> talk to pier warden

The Pier Warden squints toward the shore. 'I'd mind the high stones if I were you. …'

Pier Warden

'Nothing to say? Then it's a bad season, and it'll pass. It always has.'

Said once; your silence is the comforting lie. After that he talks as today, and the truth can still be told.
