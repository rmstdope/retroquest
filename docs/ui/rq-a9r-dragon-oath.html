Act III — the Dragon’s oath, agreed

The scale is won by finishing the old oath from the scrolls with an answer about giving back. Dashed outline = new text; everything else is today's.

1 · Reading the scrolls (any time, again and again)
> examine old oath scrolls

Brittle parchment bound with faded golden thread. Most of the script has flaked away. One line survives, written in many hands, one beneath another: “I take what is given, and I return ___.” Each hand finished it with a word of its own, and every one of those words has worn away.

The scrolls no longer crumble; they stay in the bag or room.
2 · Saying anything before the Dragon’s memory
> say hello to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles:

ancient dragon

Words come after memory, young one. Sit, and hear me first.

3 · Saying anything before the scrolls are read
> say everything to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon’s golden eyes narrow.

ancient dragon

An oath is not something you make up, child. The old words are kept in the Echo Chambers. Read them, then come back.

Even a right answer.
4 · Talking, scrolls read, oath not yet given
> talk to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles softly:

ancient dragon

You have read the old words. Then tell me: what will you give back, for what you take?

Every talk ends with the question. Before the scrolls: today’s closing line.
5 · A taking answer
> say gold to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles:

ancient dragon

Then you would be taking.

gold, silver, coin, coins, money, nothing, thanks, thank you.
6 · Anything else that does not fill the blank
> say i will give it back to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles:

ancient dragon

That is no answer, young one. What will you give back, for what you take?

Also “say oath”.
7 · The answer
> say everything to ancient dragon

You speak the old words to the ancient dragon: “I take what is given, and I return everything.”

ancient dragon

Your words ring true, young one. The scale is yours to bear. May you carry its burden with honor.

Accepted: everything / it / myself / what I take — alone, as “I return …”, or the whole line. Case, spaces and a final . or ! ignored. The word goes into the echo.
8 · After the oath, before taking the scale
> talk to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles softly:

ancient dragon

The oath has been given and accepted. The scale is yours to claim.

> say everything to ancient dragon

The ancient dragon rumbles:

ancient dragon

The oath has been given and accepted. The scale is yours to claim.

Today’s words.
9 · Mending the sigils after taking the scale
> cast bless on sigils

You cast bless on the sigils.

Light runs along the cracked ring like water finding its course. The sigils brighten, steady, and hold.

The ancient dragon lowers its head.

ancient dragon

You said you would return what you took. Most forget by the time they reach the door. Your parents would have done the same.
