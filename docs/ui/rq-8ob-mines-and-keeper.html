Examine Tobin / young miner / boy
› examine young miner

A boy of perhaps fifteen sits apart from the others on an upturned bucket, knees drawn up, a cracked lamp in his lap. His throat is wrapped in a strip of torn shirt. He watches the ceiling, not you.

Talk to Tobin — first
› talk to tobin

Tobin looks at you for a long moment. His lips part, then close again. He turns the cracked lamp over in his hands and goes back to watching the ceiling.

Talk to Tobin — second
› talk to tobin

Tobin swallows, and you see what it costs him. No sound comes. He shakes his head, once.

Talk to Tobin — third and every later time
› talk to boy

Tobin does not look up. His thumb traces the crack in the lamp's glass, back and forth.

Miners — every talk after the Dragon's Memory
› talk to miners

The lead miner leans on his pick, but he isn't working: 'You hear it too, don't you? The quiet. The picks stopped ringing. I've worked this seam thirty years and the rock always talked back — creaks, drips, the little groans before a fall. Now it's gone dead. Like something down here forgot how to breathe.'

Silence Keeper — first talk
› talk to silence keeper

The Silence Keeper whispers, their voice barely disturbing the air: 'I have kept this hush for forty years, so the sleeper below can rest. I was a singer once, in the villages above. I sang one night too loud, too near the deep, and something old turned over in its sleep. I put my voice down that night and never picked it up.'

'The stones in this room were cut to carry a whisper to the sleeper without waking it. They have gone cold. Bless them, and give them the old chant, and the way will open.'

Silence Keeper — later talks, before the way opens
› talk to silence keeper

The Silence Keeper does not turn: 'Forty years I have listened to those stones. They are cold. Bless them; give them the chant.'

Silence Keeper — after the way to the dragon has opened
› talk to silence keeper

The Silence Keeper bows their hooded head: 'The sleeper spoke with you, and the stone held. Forty years, and I have never been so glad to have kept a quiet.'
