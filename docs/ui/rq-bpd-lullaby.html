One tune: the shell, the phantoms and the dream voice

Agreed design. Highlighted text is new. Optional; nothing is gated on it, and no one names whose tune it is.

examine shell
› examine shell

A small, iridescent shell smoothed by long tides. It's pretty and shines in the moonlight. Held close, it seems to hum.

first listen (or use shell)
› listen to shell

You hold the shell to your ear. Beneath the hush of the sea, a woman is humming: a slow tune that climbs three notes and settles home again. You almost know how it goes on. When you lower the shell, the tune stays with you a moment longer.

later listens (or use shell)
› listen to shell

The woman in the shell is still humming the same slow tune.

phantoms · shell not heard
› talk to phantoms

The phantoms turn toward you, their voices rising in a chorus of whispered syllables that seem to flow like water over stone. The words are just beyond comprehension, as if spoken in a language forgotten by time.

Beneath the whispers, a few of them are humming: a slow tune that climbs three notes and settles home again.

Through the murmur, one word comes clear, over and over: rest … rest …

Dragon · heard the shell (with or without phantoms)
› talk to dragon

Then, beneath the dragon's breathing, another voice: the whisper from your dream, at the very edge of hearing. “Elior… little one. Help me. I cannot hold it shut alone.” You know it the way you know a song someone sang to you before you had words for it. And under the words, faint as the sea in a shell, runs the tune the woman hummed. Then it is gone.

You look up at the ancient dragon. Its golden eyes are half closed.

“The friend who stayed behind.”

It says no more.
