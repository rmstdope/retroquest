Option B — recommended

Shared picture style — the list plus a positive world rule

What the picture is asked to show

look: …(unchanged)… a pre-industrial world where everything is handmade: light comes only from the sun, fire, candles and oil lamps; people wear wool, linen and leather; roofs are thatch, slate or timber …(rest unchanged)… no text, lettering, signature or watermark anywhere … keepOut: photorealism, 3d render, anime, neon colours, modern objects, electric lights, light switches, wall sockets, lamp posts, telegraph or power poles, wires, TV aerials, satellite dishes, modern clothing, zips, logos, printed shirts, baseball caps, rubber boots, letters, words, writing or symbols of any kind unless the description asks for them

Why / what it costs

The same forbidden list, plus one sentence describing the world positively — firelight, wool and leather, thatch. Telling the painter what to use works better than only telling it what to avoid.

Cost: applies to every picture in every act the next time each is repainted — a slightly more "old-world" look across the board.

Option A — recommended

Riverbank — the fisherman, empty-handed

What the picture is asked to show

A gentle river flows past the village and makes a slow bend here, past the reeds and a broad, flat stone at the water's edge. A couple of insects hum over the water. A weathered old fisherman in a homespun wool smock and a shapeless felt hood sits on an upturned crate at the bend, watching the water, his bad arm held close to his side. His hands are empty: no rod, no line, no net. Beyond the reeds, only thatched cottages and trees.

Why / what it costs

Matches the story: in the game he has a bad arm and cannot fish, and the rod is something the player finds in the old shed and brings to him. A picture with no rod tells the same story the words tell.

Cost: none beyond the bug report's wording, which suggested a rod beside him.

Option B — recommended

Village Chapel — worn but readable

What the picture is asked to show

… Over the door, a small carving cut into the stone: a tree under two moons, its edges worn soft with age but the shape still clear. No other carving, letters, writing or symbols anywhere on the walls or the door.

Why / what it costs

Keeps the old, worn feel while a player who looks closely can still make out the tree and the two moons — and match it to the crest they find later.

Cost: a little less "almost smooth" than the text says.

Agreed

Borin, Elior's Cottage and the title screen

Borin: a rough undyed linen shirt, sleeves rolled past the elbow, under a scorched leather apron; forearms like fence posts, old burns on his knuckles. Elior's Cottage: lit only by the hearth fire; bare plaster and timber walls with no fittings on them. Title screen: thatched and slate roofs with stone chimneys and a curl of smoke; nothing on the roofs but chimneys.
