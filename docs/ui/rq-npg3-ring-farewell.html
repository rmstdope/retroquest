The ring, as Grandmother knows it

The agreed experience for Grandmother's ring lines. The farewell's ring line follows what she has seen or heard. The ring in the well stays a mystery for a later act; Grandmother notices once that it is odd. Dimmed lines are unchanged; highlighted lines are new.

Farewell — traded, never shown (golden path, unchanged)
› use map

Footsteps hurry along the road behind you.
Your grandmother has walked all the way out from the village. She tugs the travel cloak straight on your shoulders.

"Wear your mother's cloak. She'd want you warm."

"The merchant showed me the ring he had of you. It was your mother's wedding ring, child. No, don't fret; she'd have given it for your boots in a heartbeat."

"Old Hemlock is telling the whole square how you found his Patches."

Her hand finds the amulet at your chest and closes around it. "This was theirs, you know. Aren and Lysa's." …

She kisses your brow, and turns for home before you can see her cry. … the chapel bell rings once, with no hand on the rope.

Farewell — kept, never shown: she sees it now
› use map

Footsteps hurry along the road behind you.
Your grandmother has walked all the way out from the village. She tugs the travel cloak straight on your shoulders.

"Wear your mother's cloak. She'd want you warm."

Her eyes catch on your hand, closed around something. She opens your fingers, and the ring lies in your palm.

"Your mother's."

You tell her where you found it: at the bottom of the well. She looks at it a long moment.

"She never took it off. Not once."

She closes your fingers over it again.

"Then keep it. Let a little of her go with you."

"Old Hemlock is telling the whole square how you found his Patches."

Her hand finds the amulet at your chest and closes around it. "This was theirs, you know. Aren and Lysa's." …

She kisses your brow, and turns for home before you can see her cry. … the chapel bell rings once, with no hand on the rope.

Cottage — giving her the ring, first time
› give ring to grandmother

Your grandmother takes the ring, and her hands go still on it. "Your mother's. Your father walked to Greendale and back in a day to fetch that."

You tell her where you found it. She looks up at you, and back at the ring.

"She never took it off. Not once."

She turns it once, so the letters inside catch the light, and gives it back. "Keep it, love. That one doesn't go on a shelf."
