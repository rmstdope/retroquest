Act II — The archive names the Wardens of the Dawn

Agreed. The Great Hall archive and the Ancient Chronicle are read as pages: the Wardens' sigil, a grey-cloaked stranger asking after Willowbrook's birth rolls on Malakar's business, and a newer-ink warning that Malakar seeks the blood of Willowbrook. Neither says the bloodline is Elior's; that is Nyx's vision.

Searching the Great Hall, guided

Desktop
> search

With the historians' guidance you work through the archive. Most of it is tax rolls and land grants, but near the oldest shelves you find two things.

The first is a page from an old history, its sigil drawn in faded gold:

Of the Wardens of the Dawn. Their mark is the sun rising over three linked rings. They kept watch over the kingdom when the old wars ended and answered to no lord but the crown. None of them has been seen in a generation, and few now remember their names.

The second is the archive's own register of visitors. The same entry appears again and again over the past year, in different hands:

A man in a grey cloak. Asked for the birth rolls of Willowbrook and the families who came there. Would not give his name. Said he came on the business of Malakar.

Malakar. You had always thought him a name from old books, a tale to frighten children. Yet someone walks into this archive in his name, asking after the families of Willowbrook. The last entry is dated the week King Alden vanished.

You open the chronicle. Pressed into the leather of its cover is the sun rising over three linked rings, the mark of the Wardens of the Dawn.

The Wardens were few, and their magic was of the old kind: to loosen what another hand has bound, to lift a working laid in malice. This they taught to those they trusted, and it is written here.
