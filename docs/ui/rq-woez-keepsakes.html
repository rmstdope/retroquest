Family keepsakes — agreed

Each of them says one particular thing about the parents, the way people do when they have kept quiet a long time, and stops there. Nothing names what Act 1 holds back. Highlighted text is new. The item stays with Elior every time, and the same answer comes each time it is handed over.

Ring → Grandmother

Today: Grandmother turns it over in her hands and gives it back. "Keep it, love. My shelves are full enough."

› give shiny ring to grandmother

Your grandmother takes the ring, and her hands go still on it. "Your mother's. Your father walked to Greendale and back in a day to fetch that." She turns it once, so the letters inside catch the light, and gives it back. "Keep it, love. That one doesn't go on a shelf."

Mother's letter → Grandmother

Today: Grandmother turns it over in her hands and gives it back. "Keep it, love. My shelves are full enough."

› give mother's letter to grandmother

She reads it standing, and then again. Her mouth goes thin at one line, and she does not say which. "That's her, all over. Telling you what to do from wherever she is."
She folds it along its creases and hands it back. "Keep it dry. And do as she says." She turns to the bread.

Mother's letter → Mira, before she gives Elior the amulet

Today: Mira regards it, then shakes her head gently. "I have no need for this, child."

› give mother's letter to mira

Mira reads it to the end, and her thumb stays on one line. "She always did trust me with more than I asked for."
She hands it back. "When you are ready, Elior. Not before. Those are her words, and they are mine."

Mother's letter → Mira, once Elior carries the amulet

Today: Mira regards it, then shakes her head gently. "I have no need for this, child."

› give mother's letter to mira

Mira reads it, then looks at the amulet you carry. "I kept it. Now you keep it."
She hands the letter back. "And mind what she says about answering questions."

The cloak and the fisherman

Travel cloak → Grandmother

Today: Grandmother turns it over in her hands and gives it back. "Keep it, love. My shelves are full enough."

› give travel cloak to grandmother

Your grandmother pushes it back into your arms. "I kept that in paper all these years so you could hand it back to me? She left it for you. Wear it."

Mother's letter → fisherman

Today: The fisherman eyes it, then shrugs. "Won't catch anything with that, lad."

› give mother's letter to fisherman

The fisherman knows the paper before he reads a word, and does not read it. "So you lifted the stone. I sat by it more years than I'll own to, lad, and never once did. Wasn't mine to."
