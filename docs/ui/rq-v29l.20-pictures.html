Option A — the story stands, the scenery bends

Where the words carry the story or a puzzle, they stay and the picture is remade. Where they only set the scene, the words move to fit the picture we already have. 4 places get new words, 9 pictures are remade.

Village Well story

The game says

“Something glints far down in the well, much too deep for hands.” — the reason the fishing rod has to be lengthened.

The picture shows

A shallow open pond with lily pads; anyone could reach in.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Remake: a narrow stone well shaft dropping into darkness, water far below.

Village Square scenery

The game says

“Half the market stalls stand empty. The people at the others keep their voices down, and stop talking when you pass.”

The picture shows

A busy, sunny market, every stall trading.

Words change to fit the picture.
“The heart of Willowbrook: cobblestones around an old stone fountain, and a weathered notice board covered in faded announcements. The market is busy, but the talk at the stalls is low, and it stops when you pass.”

General Store — shut story

The game says

“A cluttered shop with the shutters half down. Rope, apples and trinkets crowd the shelves in the gloom. Hemlock stands at the window, watching the lane to the chapel.” (until the cat comes home)

The picture shows

Bright, open shop; Hemlock at the counter sorting apples. The same picture serves both shut and open.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Add a shut picture: shutters half down, dim, Hemlock at the window. The open shop keeps today’s picture.

General Store — cat home scenery

The game says

“Patches curls up beside the till and begins to purr.” and later “…looks at the counter, where his cat sleeps.”

The picture shows

The cat curled on the floor, not by the till. (It is also plain black — see the shed.)

Words change to fit the picture.
“Patches curls up on the floor by the counter and begins to purr.” and “…looks down by the counter, where his cat sleeps.”
Also for the picture: Recolour the cat to Patches’ ginger, black and white.

Abandoned Shed — inside story

The game says

Dark inside: “too dark to rummage through the clutter… you would need a light”. A “small patchwork shape” bolts behind the crates.

The picture shows

A bright, sunlit interior, and a plain black cat lying in full view on the crates.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Remake: the shed in near-darkness, light only in thin cracks; no cat in view (eyes behind the crates at most).

Abandoned Shed — the finds scenery

The game says

“Tucked away in dusty corners, you find a discarded fishing rod, and a small magnet!”

The picture shows

Rod and magnet up on the shelves, plain to see.

Words change to fit the picture.
“…Up on the dusty shelves, you find a discarded fishing rod, and a small magnet!”

Chicken Coop story

The game says

The thrown bread knocks the key “from a high rafter” and it falls to the floor.

The picture shows

Once it has fallen, the key is still drawn up on the rafter.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Move the key: lying in the straw on the floor once knocked down.

Riverbank story

The game says

You tip the smooth stone aside and dig a wax-sealed tin out of the mud; the stone then “sits askew, beside the hole you dug”.

The picture shows

A loose letter lying on the slab; the stone has not moved.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Remake: the stone tipped askew, a dug hole, a small open tin.

Forest Path story

The game says

A fox kit is caught in the snare until you cut it free (a moment of mercy).

The picture shows

An empty snare from the start.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Two pictures: the kit caught in the snare, then the cut, empty snare once it is free.

Old Mill scenery

The game says

“Dusty gears and cobwebs fill the interior… Sunlight streams through broken windows.” Jamming it: “a hidden compartment slides open, revealing a fragment of the old millstone!”

The picture shows

An outdoor yard: windmill behind, the iron wheel under a lean-to; the fragment lies on the ground.

Words change to fit the picture.
“A creaky windmill towers above, its sails turning slowly in the breeze. In the yard below, sacks of grain slump against a stone wall beside a heavy millstone, and the scent of flour hangs in the air.” · “Under the lean-to by the wall, you notice a strange mechanism of black iron…” · “With a clunk, a hidden compartment springs open, and a fragment of the old millstone drops to the ground at your feet!”

Village Chapel story

The game says

“Over the door a shape is cut into the stone, worn almost smooth.” It is the tree under two moons — the same crest as the locket, which the priest and Grandmother speak of.

The picture shows

Crisp, sharp marks (“#F XXX”) beside the door, not over it.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Remake: a faint, worn carving of a tree under two moons above the door.

Elior’s Cottage story

The game says

A faded photograph “hidden in a drawer”: “two adults and a child” — your parents, and you.

The picture shows

A framed portrait of two men, standing on the mantel.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Remake: a small faded photograph in an opened drawer; two adults and a child.

Hidden Glade — rare flower story

The game says

“A delicate, radiant flower… petals shimmer with a faint magical glow.” In the bag it is drawn purple with a gold heart.

The picture shows

Pale yellow-green in the glade; purple in the bag. The two pictures disagree with each other.

Words stay. Picture is remade.
For the picture: Repaint the glade flower deep purple with gold at its centre, faintly glowing, to match the bag.
