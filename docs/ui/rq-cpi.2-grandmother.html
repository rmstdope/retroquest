Grandmother and the dream — where she reacts, and how much she lets slip

Two questions on one page. Where: P1 puts her reaction into the talk that already follows reading the journal (every player sees it, on the way to learning revive); P2 keeps it optional, only when Elior shows her the journal. How much: G1 she deflects, with a tell; G2 she asks one question too many, then takes it back. Neither says whose voice it is. The outlined part is new; the father's tale after it is unchanged.

P1 + G1 — "only dreams"
› talk to grandmother

◆You speak with grandmother.

You tell her about the dream: the shadows, the figure, the voice calling your name, and how you were sure you had heard it before. Her teacup stops halfway to her lips.

Grandmother

Dreams, child. Storms bring them. That's all it was.

She sets the cup down carefully, and does not look at you for a while.

Grandmother

That journal of yours... your father was a great writer, you know. … (tale as today)

◆… You have learned the Revive spell!

Classic guarded denial; the tell is in what she does, not what she says. Subtle — some players will read it as plain comfort.
P1 + G2 — "did it say anything else?" (recommended)
› talk to grandmother

◆You speak with grandmother.

You tell her about the dream: the shadows, the figure, the voice calling your name, and how you were sure you had heard it before. She reaches for your hand and holds it tighter than she means to.

Grandmother

That voice... did it say anything more? Anything but your name?

You shake your head. She lets go, and smooths her apron.

Grandmother

No. No, of course not. Never mind an old woman's fancies.

Grandmother

That journal of yours... your father was a great writer, you know. … (tale as today)

◆… You have learned the Revive spell!

Her question gives her away — she expected the voice to say something — and then she covers. Clearly "she knows more" without naming anyone.
P2 — only when shown the journal (optional)
› give journal to grandmother

◆You show the journal to grandmother.

She reads the page about the dream, and her lips move with the words. …(G1 or G2 reaction)

She closes the book and hands it back.

Rewards a curious player, but most will never try it: the reaction this piece of work is about would be missed. Today, offering her the journal gets her generic "looks at it" line.
