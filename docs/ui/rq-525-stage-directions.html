Option A — the direction steps out of the quote as narration

A stage direction becomes an ordinary line of story text between quotes, the way a novel writes an action beat. The quote is split around it; the speaker's label shows only on the first part.

Today (for reference)
› talk to training master
Training Master

Ah, a visitor to our training grounds. I've been instructing knights and squires here for many years. *He glances toward Sir Cedric with a troubled expression.* It pains me to see a fine knight like Sir Cedric carrying such a heavy burden. There are whispers about his past military service…

Training Master

*He leans closer, lowering his voice.* I've heard rumors that there were supposed to be documents - evidence that would clear his name…

› talk to barmaid elena
Barmaid Elena

*coughs weakly* Hello, traveler. I apologize for my appearance…

The asterisks and the direction are read in the speaker's italic voice, as if spoken.
A direction in the middle of speech
› talk to training master
Training Master

Ah, a visitor to our training grounds. I've been instructing knights and squires here for many years.

He glances toward Sir Cedric with a troubled expression.

It pains me to see a fine knight like Sir Cedric carrying such a heavy burden. There are whispers about his past military service…

He leans closer, lowering his voice.

I've heard rumors that there were supposed to be documents - evidence that would clear his name…

The quote ends, the beat is told as narration, the same speaker carries on without a second label.
A direction that opens the speech
› talk to barmaid elena

Elena coughs weakly.

Barmaid Elena

Hello, traveler. I apologize for my appearance…

A fragment like coughs weakly needs a subject to stand as a sentence: it would be rewritten as Elena coughs weakly. — the same goes for beaming with health and vitality, weakly, tears of joy in his eyes. Those exact words get agreed next.

My friend, I cannot express how grateful I am…

Please accept this Nature's Charm…

Already narration today, only with asterisks around it; it simply loses them.
