Act III — the Dragon names the dream voice and echoes the refrain

Agreed. On the first talk the Dragon's memory now ends with the refrain. The amulet glows, the dream voice whispers once as italic words inside a narration paragraph with no speaker label, and the Dragon names it “the friend who stayed behind” and says no more. A save that heard the memory before this change hears the voice once, on its next talk.

The first talk

Desktop
> talk to dragon

The Ancient Dragon's voice resonates through your mind like distant thunder:

Ancient Dragon

Young one, I remember your parents well. Aren and Lysa, Wardens of the Dawn. They came to me with their friend Malakar, the three of them laughing, and made the relics as equals.

When Malakar tore a wound in time, it was they who stood against him. They poured themselves into a ward. It hides you from his sight, and it holds his wound shut. It is anchored in the amulet you carry, and in the bindings of your king. That is why Malakar drains him.

They are not gone as the dead are gone, child. They are warded away, not dead.

Ancient Dragon

They had a saying, the three of them, carved wherever they worked: Power follows purpose; purpose follows truth.

The Ancient Amulet blazes in your hand a third time. Aren stands beside Lysa now, hand in hand. Behind them, the third figure still has his back turned.

(the mines fall silent — that line is agreed in a separate piece of work and sits here)

Then, beneath the dragon's breathing, another voice: the whisper from your dream, at the very edge of hearing. “Elior… little one. Help me. I cannot hold it shut alone.” You know it the way you know a song someone sang to you before you had words for it. Then it is gone.

You look up at the Ancient Dragon. Its golden eyes are half closed.

Ancient Dragon

The friend who stayed behind.

It says no more.

The Ancient Dragon rumbles softly:

Ancient Dragon

The memory has been shared. Your path leads forward now, not back. The scale awaits when you are ready to prove your selflessness.
